The challenge,
without the fog.

You get short, incomplete reads of someone’s DNA. Turn their overlaps into votes, work out which alleles sit on the same chromosome copy, then see whether a quantum circuit can sample good answers.

In one sentence: rebuild the two haplotypes of five SNPs, using QAOA to search a tiny optimization problem.

DNA has 4 letters. Why is one bit enough?

A key only needs the letters that actually show up at that SNP. In this lab that is always exactly two, and two options fit in one bit.

SNP 1SNP 2
seen in readsT, CA, G
never seen thereA, GC, T
key (1 bit)T=0 C=1A=0 G=1
  • Why only two? A SNP usually comes from one mutation long ago: the original letter plus the changed one. Sites with 3 or 4 letters are less common, and this simplified lab picks only two-letter (biallelic) SNPs.
  • Which letter gets 0? Any fixed choice works. Real tools use 0 for the letter in the standard reference genome.
  • Do I convert letters myself? No. The lab’s READS already arrive as 0, 1 and −1 (missing).
Now scale up: the notebook uses five SNPs. The same moves give copy A = 01101 and copy B = 10010, the hidden pair at the top of this page.

Where the copies differ

Variant
A position where DNA differs, between people or between your two copies.
one line in a diff
SNPsay “snip”
Single-nucleotide polymorphism: a variant that changes just one letter. The lab uses five known SNPs.
a one-character diff at a known index

One version of the letter at a SNP, e.g. T or C at SNP 1 in the pictures. The lab writes the two versions as 0 and 1 using a fixed key per SNP; they are labels, not DNA letters.

Only two of the four DNA letters ever appear at that SNP, so one bit can label them.

Heterozygous
Your two copies carry different alleles at that SNP. Every lab SNP is heterozygous, so each column has one 0 and one 1.
the two versions always disagree here

The puzzle

Haplotype
The alleles along one copy, read in order, for example 01101.
bit vector x; the other copy is 1 − x
Phaseand “phasing”
Which alleles sit together on the same copy. Phasing means reconstructing the two haplotypes.
assign each column’s two values to the correct row
Cis / trans
Two alternate (1) alleles on the same copy are in cis; on different copies, in trans. If two variants both damage one gene, cis leaves the other copy intact, while trans can affect both. The lab makes no medical predictions.
x[i] == x[j] versus x[i] != x[j]

1 · Understand the puzzle

One chromosome pair. A lot of missing pieces.

The person has two chromosome copies. The lab looks at five SNPs; each is biallelic and heterozygous, so every position has one 0 and one 1 across the pair.

What does “phase” mean?

Phase asks which alleles are together on the same copy. At two sites, 11 / 00 means the 1s are together (cis). 10 / 01 means they are on opposite copies (trans).

The 0/1 symbols are labels, not actual DNA letters. This toy exercise is not a medical diagnosis.

Why two answers can mean the same thing

We name one haplotype x; the other is automatically 1 − x. If we swap the copy names, every bit flips, but “same” and “opposite” relationships stay the same.

00110 and 11001 describe the same pairing

Reads are already aligned to known SNP positions. The challenge is not finding SNPs or assembling raw DNA.

2 · Reads become votes

Every overlapping pair leaves a clue.

Ignore missing positions. For each read, compare every pair of positions it does cover. Equal bits vote “same”; different bits vote “opposite.”

opposite vote same vote

Votes add up; they aren’t certainty.

For each site pair (i, j), keep two counts:

CountMeaning
A[i,j]reads saying the pair is the same
D[i,j]reads saying the pair is opposite

The starter counts every pair in a read, even non-neighboring SNPs. One bad call can affect several pairs, so these votes are correlated—not independent proof.

3 · Score a proposed answer

A candidate loses points when it disagrees with the clues.

The notebook’s tiny example has conflicting votes. Try changing the candidate bits to see how its penalty changes.

In words: agreeing with the pair’s majority avoids a penalty; disagreeing pays for the votes you contradict.

Try 011 (cost 1), then 001 (cost 4). Lower is better for this model.

Important: “best score” means best fit to this vote-counting rule. It does not prove which chromosome arrangement is biologically true.
4 · Turn votes into an Ising model

Same problem, new notation.

Quantum circuits often express optimization with spins instead of bits. Nothing about the puzzle changes; the new form is convenient for quantum gates.

Bit to spin

zᵢ = 1 − 2xᵢ   →   bit 0 becomes +1, bit 1 becomes −1

Each pair gets a coupling J = (D − A) / 2. A negative J favors equal bits; a positive J favors different bits.

Why an offset appears

energy = offset + Σ Jᵢⱼ zᵢ zⱼ

The offset makes the energy numerically match the vote cost. It is constant across candidates, so it doesn’t change which answer is best.

The notebook checks that the two scores match for all candidates in the tiny example before building a circuit.

5 · QAOA searches candidates

Build a quantum sampler, tune it classically.

QAOA is a loop: prepare many possible bit strings at once, use the graph to change their quantum phases, mix possibilities, then adjust angles to make low-cost answers more likely.

The cost layer nudges relative phases; the mixer lets amplitudes move between bit strings. Interference changes which answers are sampled more often.

Start broadly

Hadamard gates prepare an even distribution over all possible bit strings.

Use the clues

RZZ cost gates encode pair relationships; RX mixer gates move probability around. One cost-plus-mixer pair is a layer.

Adjust and sample

A classical optimizer tunes the angles. Then fresh measurements produce candidate answers to score.

Reality check: the challenge does not assume quantum advantage. For five SNPs, all 32 answers can be checked classically; that exact answer is the baseline.
6 · Read the results honestly

“Best sample” and “typical sample” are different.

Measure both how good the whole set of samples is and how often the circuit found the minimum-cost candidates.

Mean cost = typical quality

Average the costs of all measured answers. Lower mean cost suggests the distribution tends to favor better answers.

Optimum frequency = repeatability

What fraction of samples landed on any minimum-cost answer? One lucky minimum is less convincing than finding it repeatedly.

Fair baseline: compare QAOA with uniform random sampling and exact enumeration. The notebook uses 2,048 final shots; its training uses a statevector simulator, not training shots.
7 · Hardware and noise

Same math, messier physical circuit.

A real device can only connect qubits in certain ways. Transpilation maps logical qubits onto the device, adds routing gates if needed, and turns abstract gates into supported operations.

What the lab compares

  • Ideal simulation
  • A noisy simulation using a historical FakeManila device snapshot
  • Optional: the same fixed circuit on an IBM QPU

Keep the experiment fair

To isolate hardware effects, keep the reads, cost function, trained angles and shot count the same. Report circuit depth and two-qubit gate count too.

A fake device is a useful noise model, not a live quantum computer. One run cannot cleanly separate noise from ordinary shot-to-shot variation.

Qiskit bit-order warning: printed measurement keys put qubit 0 on the right, while the notebook writes SNP 1 first. Use the supplied decoder once; don’t reverse the bits again after routing.

What this model can’t promise

Optimization finds the best answer to the model—not necessarily reality.

Missing clues

If reads never connect two groups of SNPs, their relative phase may be impossible to infer from the available evidence.

Conflicting or biased votes

Errors, correlated pairs, and equal-weight assumptions can make a minimum-score answer differ from biological truth.

Small experiment

One tiny dataset, one random seed, or one favorable sample distribution cannot establish general usefulness or a speed advantage.
